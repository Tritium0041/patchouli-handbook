# Glossary

在深入具体 entry 前，先在这里建立频道反复出现的术语语境。

## action stages
The four phases of an attack animation: idle, wind-up, attack, and follow-through.

## analogy trap
The tendency to describe new games by comparing them to existing ones, which can mislead and stifle originality.

## animation blending
Technique of smoothly transitioning between two or more animations by mixing their poses. Essential for natural movement.

## backlog cost
The perceived time investment required to complete a game, which can deter players if emphasized in marketing.

## billboard
A polygon that always faces the camera, commonly used for particles, explosions, and simplified objects.

## BPM
Beats Per Minute: a measure of music tempo used to match the pace of gameplay.

## button configuration
Player-customizable key bindings that improve accessibility and comfort, especially in multiplayer settings.

## cancel frame
A frame in an animation where the player can input to cancel the current action into another, improving responsiveness.

## center of gravity shift
Deliberately offsetting the rotation pivot point of an object from its geometric center to create a more organic, unstable feeling in animations.

## character silhouette
The distinct outline of a character that makes it recognizable and readable, especially important in fast-paced games.

## collision (attack collision)
The area or volume that determines when an attack connects with an opponent, often larger than the visual model for playability.

## color blindness filter
A tool simulating different types of color vision deficiency to test game visual accessibility.

## command input
A specific sequence of controller directions and button presses to execute a special move.

## concept board (image board)
A visual reference used to unify art style across a team, showing backgrounds, characters, and mood.

## convergent design
Design focused on a single peak achievement like high score or fastest time.

## cutscene skip
The ability to bypass or fast-forward story sequences to respect player time and maintain pacing.

## daily report (nippō)
An internal journal shared by the director to communicate news, issues, and progress with the team daily.

## debug mode
Developer-only features for testing and tuning (e.g., invincibility, camera control). Usually removed from the final product.

## difficulty curve
The pattern of challenge over time in a game, ideally with peaks (hard sections) and valleys (easy/exploration sections), not linear.

## difficulty escalation
The practice of gradually increasing challenge while providing options to lower difficulty or skip obstacles to avoid player frustration.

## display latency
The delay between an input and its visual representation on screen, which can critically harm gameplay feel.

## divergent design
Design philosophy that encourages multiple paths and emergent outcomes rather than a single optimal solution.

## DPCM
Differential Pulse-Code Modulation; a sampling audio format used on the Famicom for playback of recorded sounds.

## e-conte
Electronic storyboard; a detailed visual plan for CG animation sequences.

## echo chamber
A closed environment where similar opinions are reinforced, making alternative views invisible or suppressed.

## exaggeration
Amplifying actions in animation to compensate for the loss of detail in polygon characters.

## family BASIC
A Famicom peripheral released in 1984 that allowed users to write programs in BASIC, using the console as a simple computer.

## flashing
A visual effect that makes a character or screen briefly brighten, often used to indicate invincibility or state changes.

## flick input
An input method using analog stick flick speed to control action intensity, such as dash or run speed, offering intuitive and emotional control.

## flipped animation
Mirroring character sprites or models so that opponents face each other toward the screen, enhancing visual communication in fighting games.

## follow-through
Recovery animation after an attack that conveys impact and occupies most of the attack animation time.

## forward kinematics (FK)
Animation technique where motion is driven from the root of a skeleton outward to extremities.
